This story isn't your typical one

The grandfather clock in the foyer did not chime on the hour; it exhaled.

Julian had noticed it three days after moving into the estate at Blackwood End. At 12:00, the pendulum would freeze mid-swing, the iron hands would quiver, and a long, cool sigh would rustle the velvet drapes near the grand staircase.

On the fourth night, Julian stood before the towering mahogany case, candle in hand. The flame flickered violently as the minute hand nudged the twelve.



The draft smelled of river mud and crushed lavender.

"Who are you?" Julian whispered, feeling silly as the words left his mouth.

The glass door of the clock unlatched itself with a soft click and swung open. Inside, behind the heavy brass weights, there were no gears. There were no springs or pendulum rods. Instead, a narrow, spiral staircase made of tarnished silver wound down into the darkness beneath the floorboards.

Taking a breath, Julian set his candlestick on the side table and stepped through the frame. The descent was steep, the silver steps cold beneath his boots. Down he went, turning in tight circles until the sounds of the stormy night above vanished entirely.

He reached the bottom and stepped into a subterranean library lit by floating, jarred fireflies. Thousands of leather-bound volumes lined walls made of living tree roots. At the center of the room sat an elderly woman in a velvet armchair, meticulously repairing the binding of a massive book with golden thread.

She looked up, her eyes the color of aged parchment. "You're late," she said, her voice carrying the exact cadence of the clock's breath. "The previous owner was far more punctual with the tea."

Julian smiled, reaching for an empty chair. "I apologize. I was waiting for the right time."
